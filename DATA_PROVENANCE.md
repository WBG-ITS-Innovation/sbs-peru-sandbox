# Data provenance

Every complaint record committed to this repository at `HEAD` is **generated**.
Each one comes from a seeded script in `scripts/` that reads no external data,
so anyone with a clone can reproduce the committed artifact byte-for-byte and
check that claim rather than take it. Nothing at `HEAD` is, or is derived from,
a real consumer complaint or a real person.

Complaint data reached this project in two forms and both are gone from `HEAD`.
The SBS shared two sample workbooks under a private arrangement; they were
never committed and are gitignored at `.gitignore:102`. Extracts of them
**were** committed, and were replaced with generated equivalents on
2026-08-14 — see [What changed, and why](#what-changed-and-why) below, which
also covers what remains in git history.

## What generates what

| Committed artifact | Generated by | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| `data/synthetic-corpus-golden/` | [`scripts/generate-synthetic-corpus.py`](scripts/generate-synthetic-corpus.py) `--golden` | 200 Annex 1-A rows for each of three fictional demo institutions, plus a manifest per institution recording row count, reporting period, schema version and a SHA-256 of the CSV. Narratives come from the templates in [`data/synthetic-corpus-templates.yaml`](data/synthetic-corpus-templates.yaml). Regenerate with `make corpus-golden`. |
| `app/src/lib/rr1-2025.json` | [`scripts/build_rr1_fixture.py`](scripts/build_rr1_fixture.py) | The RR1 *reglamento de reclamos* fixture behind `/app/rr1`: three sheets (Empresa / Producto / Motivo) of monthly complaint volumes. Institution labels are fictional; every count is a seeded draw over an invented grand total. |
| `app/src/lib/journey-emails.json` | [`scripts/build_journey_fixtures.py`](scripts/build_journey_fixtures.py) | 80 complaint records with claim and resolution narratives, behind the FI inbox, the triage and submit screens, and the live-ingestion ticker. Generated from 15 producto/motivo case types, each with its own hand-written Spanish templates. |
| `app/src/lib/golden-complaint.json` | [`scripts/seed_golden_complaint.py`](scripts/seed_golden_complaint.py) | Scores and feature attributions for the one complaint the demo walks through end to end. |
| `scripts/dev-seed.sql` | hand-written | The three demo institutions, their identifiers and sandbox credentials. |
| `app/src/lib/source-samples.ts` | hand-written | Placeholder cross-source cards (social / INDECOPI / SBS DSC). Every card rendering these carries a *datos de muestra* badge and the URLs are obviously fake. |
| `api/sbs_api/agents/fixtures/replay/` | hand-written | Recorded agent responses for the `replay` provider, written against the generated corpus. |

All three generators take a `--seed` (default `2026`) and stamp no wall-clock
time into their output, so regeneration is byte-stable and a diff means
something changed on purpose. The reproduction check is:

```bash
python scripts/build_rr1_fixture.py
python scripts/build_journey_fixtures.py
make corpus-golden
git diff --exit-code          # clean
```

`tests/cleanup/test_no_real_entities.py` gates the property this document
asserts: no real financial institution is named in any committed data fixture
or generator.

## What is not generated: the regulatory vocabulary

Two things in these fixtures are real, and neither is data.

**Taxonomy labels.** The product categories and complaint motives that label
the RR1 sheets, and the `PRD_SBS` / `MOT_SBS` values on complaint rows, are the
code-list of the *reglamento de reclamos* — the category names a supervisor
reads off a SUCAVE report. They are the vocabulary the volumes are counted
into, not the counts. The demo would not be recognisable without them, and
`sbs_api.taxonomy.dictionary_v1` exists to normalise their surface forms.

**Entity classes.** The Empresa sheet's five heading rows — Banco, Financiera,
Caja municipal, Caja rural, Empresa de Crédito — are regulatory classes of
supervised entity, not entities. The fictional institutions sit beneath them.

## Fictional identities

Institutions are invented, and the same invention runs through the whole
sandbox: `scripts/dev-seed.sql` seeds *Banco Nuevo Horizonte del Perú*,
*Cooperativa de Ahorro Coopac Andes Centro* and *Financiera Surandina del
Perú* against the identifiers `SBS-001234` / `SBS-005678` / `SBS-009012`, and
the RR1 fixture names the same three among its 45 fictional entities. The
legacy code forms `BANCO_DEMO_001`, `COOPAC_DEMO_002` and
`FINANCIERA_DEMO_003` refer to the same three. The leading word of each name
(*Banco* / *Cooperativa* / *Financiera*) is load-bearing —
`sbs_api.peer_risk.cohorts` derives a segment from it.

Person-like names in complaint records and narratives are **assembled** from
pools of Peruvian given names and surnames held in the generator, one draw per
row. A name in the data is a combination, not a person. Demo personas and
login accounts use role names (*Conduct Supervisor (Demo)*) and addresses under
`sandbox.example.com`, a domain RFC 2606 reserves for documentation and
testing.

Document numbers, telephone numbers, amounts, dates, card terminals, contract
numbers and office numbers are all drawn from the seed. Document numbers are
format-valid — 8-digit DNI, 11-digit RUC with a correct Sunat check digit —
because the API's Annex 1-A validation rules check the format and a fixture
that could not pass them would not exercise the pipeline.

## Deliberate data-quality faults

`app/src/lib/journey-emails.json` carries a bounded set of defects on purpose,
because the data-quality engine is a feature of this project and a clean
fixture gives it nothing to find:

- The same logical value spelled differently between the two sheets
  (`Página web de la empresa` against `PAG. WEB DE LA EMPRESA`), plus a few
  terms deliberately absent from the taxonomy dictionary so the
  `taxonomy-unknown-term` warning path fires.
- Ubigeo codes that are blank, zero-filled, three- and four-digit, and one
  stored as a number rather than a string.
- Small-entity rows carrying a twelve-digit core-banking client number while
  declaring the document type as `DNI`, which is what `DQ-A1A-009` catches.

The generator docstring says which of these is which, so a reader does not have
to guess whether a fault is intentional.

## PII-bearing fields are deliberately not modeled

The Anexo 1-A complaint schema — 27 fields under Res. SBS N° 04036-2022, being
23 base fields plus 4 conditional bancaseguros fields — includes PII-bearing
fields such as the complainant's national identity document number and full
name. In the **API's** curated subset these are **not modeled**: an age-range
bucket (for example, `25-34`) stands in, so supervisory analytics run without
handling personal data. The reasoning is in
[ADR 0026](docs/adr/0026-anexo-1a-curated-subset.md).

The demo-journey fixture is the deliberate exception. Its FI-side rows carry
names and document numbers, in the structured columns and inside the narrative
free text, because the demo's whole point at that step is watching the SBS
receive path redact them. Those values are generated, as described above.

## What changed, and why

Until 2026-08-14 two committed fixtures were extracts of the SBS sample
workbooks rather than generated, and this document said otherwise. A
pre-publication sweep found both:

- `app/src/lib/rr1-2025.json` held month-by-month 2025 complaint volumes
  attributed to 50 named, real, regulated Peruvian entities.
- `app/src/lib/journey-emails.json` held 80 real consumer-complaint narratives.
  Identifiers in them had been substituted and that substitution worked, but
  the prose had not been rewritten — it carried the dates, amounts, card
  terminals and circumstances of real disputes — and the substitution wrote
  four named real banks into that text.

Both were replaced by the generated equivalents described above, on the same
day the sweep reported, and both generators lost their ability to read a
workbook at all. `tests/cleanup/test_no_real_entities.py` was added so the
property cannot silently regress.

**Git history is a separate question and is not covered by the claim at the top
of this document.** The commits before that replacement contain the earlier
fixtures, and other pre-publication findings are also history-only. Whether and
how history is published — squash, rewrite, or fresh branch — is an open
decision recorded in the audit trail under `docs/audit/`, not something this
document settles.

## Demo credentials

Credentials in `docker-compose.yaml` and the seed scripts are sandbox-only
defaults (for example, the Postgres and Keycloak admin logins). They exist so
the local stack starts with one command. They are not secrets and must not be
reused outside a local sandbox. Production secrets come from Helm values, not
from this repository.

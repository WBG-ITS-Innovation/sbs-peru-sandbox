## Context

The SBS Peru SupTech engagement has a sprint kickoff on May 25 in Lima. The audience for the demonstration is the SBS policy and technical reviewers and the WBG delivery team. (Named attendees are kept in meeting notes, not in this ADR — role labels age better than person-by-person enumeration.) The engagement has a firm deadline of July 28, 2026 (Peru's presidential inauguration day), with the incoming administration's mandate to set the cutoff.

PLAN.md as currently structured lists 11 Parts with exit criteria. Nine days separate this decision from May 25. The 11-Part scope is the July deliverable, not the May 25 deliverable. Without an explicit scope-narrowing decision, the implicit expectation drifts toward "ship everything in PLAN.md by May 25," which is unachievable in nine days and would harm demonstration quality by spreading effort across surfaces that don't earn trust at sprint kickoff.

The deliverable on May 25 is a demonstration that the architecture is real, the API works on both ingestion tiers, the agents produce credible supervisory output, and the path to July production deployment is articulated. This does not require shipping every Part. It requires shipping the parts that earn trust at sprint kickoff and articulating the rest as a credible roadmap.

## Decision

The May 25 sprint kickoff demonstration ships:

**Full scope:**
- Part 2 (Data Model & API Skeleton).
- Part 3 (Ingestion Tier 1).
- Part 4 (Ingestion Tier 2 + Synthetic Data). Full scope in the May 25 demonstration if all prompts land on schedule; reclassified to post-sprint roadmap in the fallback case (see Consequences). Cut line if Tier 2 slips: ship Tier 1 only with an explicit "Tier 2 batch deferred to post-sprint" card in the demo deck.

**Reduced scope:**
- Part 5 (ML Substrate): pretrained BETO classifier only. No fine-tuning, no XGBoost ranker, no SHAP, no MLflow. The classifier interface is demonstrated; the full ML pipeline is post-sprint.
- Part 6 (Agent Infrastructure): two agents live (pattern detection + supervisory query authoring); three agents (institutional risk + conduct + investigation) ship pre-generated output. All five named and described in the UI; the three pre-generated agents render archived output. The three pre-generated agents render output that is generated by the same agent code path running against curated synthetic complaints in advance, not hand-written prose; the demonstration can show the generation command and the output file together. Architecture documents how the three pre-generated agents are wired. Stretch: all five live by end of Day 8.
- Part 7 (Developer Portal + Onboarding Tier A): Stoplight or Redoc rendering of the OpenAPI spec; OpenAPI + JSON Schema generated and version-stamped as the v0.1 standards pack. SDK generation in four languages, conformance test suite, and getting-started guide are deferred.
- Part 8 (Self-Service Onboarding + Per-Institution Ops Tier B): per-institution dashboard rendering data from the real API for at least one named institution. Self-service credential portal and full per-institution operations are deferred.
- Part 11 (Standards Pack & Reporting Taxonomy Distribution): v0.1 standards pack (OpenAPI + JSON Schema) generated and version-stamped as part of Part 7's May 25 work. Versioning policy, code lists distribution, and OCI artifact publishing are deferred.

**Deferred entirely:**
- Part 9 (Production Readiness). The demonstration runs on a Mac with Homebrew services. A brief k6 load test on Day 9 gives one illustrative number for "how does it scale." Production hardening, Helm chart, Terraform, and the full deployment substrate are June/July work.
- Part 10 (AI/ML Evaluation Framework). The demonstration uses curated synthetic data. Rigorous evaluation is July.

The PLAN.md restructure landing alongside this ADR documents what lands, what's reduced, and what's deferred per Part, with target dates for deferrals.

## Precedent

This is a scope-and-sequencing decision for a nine-day sprint. **No direct sprint-phasing or minimum-credible-demo comparator was located in [docs/research/](../research/) during this prompt.** The research files document what regulators built and how they staged it across years, not how a solo developer stages a nine-day kickoff demonstration. Sprint-phasing precedent is deferred to a future research note. **This ADR is Proposed until that comparator lands** (see Promotion criteria above, and the corresponding entry in [docs/DEFERRED.md](../DEFERRED.md)).

The following citations support the broader regulator-domain context for this work, **not** the sprint-phasing decision specifically. They are recorded here so the ADR is grounded in real comparators rather than invented ones, and so future readers can see the gap clearly.

**Complaint taxonomy comparator.** [market-comparators.md §2.1](../research/market-comparators.md#21-us-cfpb-consumer-complaint-database) documents the US CFPB Consumer Complaint Database, whose product → sub-product → issue → sub-issue taxonomy is the strongest public reference for the shape of Anexo 1-A. This grounds the regulator-domain framing of the demonstration but says nothing about how to stage a sprint.

**Complaints-reporting modernization comparator.** [market-comparators.md §2.3](../research/market-comparators.md#23-uk-fca-complaints-reporting-regime) documents the UK FCA's 2025–2026 complaints-reporting modernization (PS25/19): single unified complaints return, permission-based reporting, updated taxonomy, fixed reporting periods. This grounds the regulator-domain framing of a consolidated complaints return as the May 25 deliverable's centerpiece but, again, says nothing about sprint phasing.

## Divergence

This decision diverges from "ship everything in PLAN.md by May 25." That stance is rejected as (a) unachievable in nine calendar days for a solo developer working through a structured prompt sequence with closeout pipelines per prompt, and (b) harmful to demonstration quality, because effort spread across surfaces that don't earn trust at sprint kickoff (production hardening, full ML pipeline, SDK generation in four languages) trades against the surfaces that do (Tier 1 near-real-time ingestion end-to-end with signed, authenticated requests; in the full demonstration: Tier 2 batch upload, agents producing credible supervisory output, and the UI showing real data flowing).

The decision also diverges from informal scope management. The honesty principle in `CLAUDE.md` requires that deferrals be documented explicitly. This ADR is that documentation.

## Consequences

**PLAN.md is the authoritative record of May 25 scope.** Any drift from this scope mid-sprint requires a documented decision (in `docs/DECISIONS.md` or a new ADR), not silent re-prioritization. The deferred items have target dates (June or July) and known follow-up Parts.

**Schedule risk is real and named.** The May 25 demonstration depends on Prompts 5-8 — covering ruff/pyright tooling and Prompt 3 carry-overs (Prompt 5), OpenAPI 3.1 spec and Pydantic v2 models (Prompt 6), FastAPI scaffold with Tier 1 endpoints (Prompt 7), and authentication and signing via mTLS / OAuth client_credentials / HMAC (Prompt 8) — plus Prompts 9-13 covering Tier 2 batch + synthetic data, UI wiring against the real API, the pretrained BETO classifier, and the two live agents. If Prompts 9-13 slip, the demonstration runs against Prompts 5-8 alone: Tier 1 near-real-time ingestion (signed and authenticated, RFC 9457, idempotency) end-to-end, preserving the minimum observable chain from the vertical slice target — a signed mTLS request to `POST /v1/complaints` → validated against Annex 1-A → persisted → event emitted → audit log → trace in Grafana. Tier 2 batch upload and the synthetic data generator are framed as the post-sprint roadmap. The UI shows the Tier 1 ingestion path; bulk-load and historical backfill come post-sprint. The pretrained BETO classifier (Part 5 reduced scope) and the two live agents (Part 6 reduced scope) are also deferred to the post-sprint roadmap under this fallback; the UI shows the architecture story for these surfaces without live behaviour. This is a thinner fallback than the full demonstration and the audience expectation is calibrated to it in advance, not at the demo. If any of Prompts 5-8 itself slips, the demonstration loses the signed-ingestion floor entirely; in that case the maintainer escalates to the WBG tech lead and the WBG manager before May 25 to either compress remaining prompts, reduce Prompts 5-8 scope, or shift the demonstration to an architecture-walkthrough format. That escalation is the load-bearing risk control for the critical path.

**Deferred items have known follow-up Parts.** Production readiness (Part 9) lands in June/July. AI/ML evaluation (Part 10) lands in July. SDK generation (Part 7 full scope), self-service onboarding (Part 8 full scope), full ML substrate (Part 5 full scope), all five agents live (Part 6 full scope), versioning policy and OCI distribution (Part 11 full scope) all have target dates in the PLAN.md restructure.

**This is a deliberate, time-bounded exception to the one-command-deploy principle.** [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) north-star principle #1 requires that `helm install` on a fresh cluster produces a working system. The May 25 demonstration runs on a Mac with Homebrew services and does not meet that bar. The exception is intentional, named, and ends when Part 9 lands (June/July 2026). Until then, the deploy principle is suspended only for the May 25 demonstration; it remains in force for all other work.

**The Superintendent and executive reviewer can ask "why isn't X done by May 25" and the answer is this ADR.** That conversation is built into the process, not deferred to the demo.
